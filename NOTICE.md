## CEFR-J vocabulary library

`data/cefrj-a1-b2.json` is a transformed copy of the **CEFR-J Wordlist Version 1.6**, compiled by Yukio Tono, Tono Laboratory, Tokyo University of Foreign Studies (2020-03-24). Retrieved from the official download on 2026-11-18.

- Source and terms: https://cefr-j.org/download.html
- Original download: https://cefr-j.org/data/CEFRJ_wordlist_ver1.6.zip
- Full source ZIP checksum and retrieval date: `source` metadata in the JSON file.
- The original workbook states that research, education and commercial use are permitted free of charge with appropriate attribution. Changes and derived wordlists must acknowledge the source. Copyright remains with Tono Laboratory, TUFS; these data are **not MIT-licensed**. The project's general contact page asks commercial users to contact the project: https://cefr-j.org/contact_eng.html. Confirm the planned public/commercial use with the project before a commercial launch.

The import preserves all 7,801 word-and-part-of-speech entries: A1 1,166; A2 1,411; B1 2,445; B2 2,779. A headword with multiple parts of speech is represented by multiple entries and can have different levels. Slash-separated spelling alternatives are presented under one entry. Themes are retained when the source supplies them. IDs are derived from the original headword, part of speech and level. The source list is not a dictionary of definitions.

Original AI-generated explanations, Chinese glosses, collocations and examples are added on demand by this application and identified separately from source wordlist data. They have not been endorsed or reviewed by CEFR-J. The interface's mastery labels are the learner's self-assessment, not a CEFR proficiency certificate.
